Call for Participation

We invite teams from signal processing, machine learning, clinical monitoring, anesthesia, neuroscience, and consciousness research. The challenge asks participants to forecast a clinically observed response to propofol from information available at the time of administration. Training data will be public. Final evaluation will use an independent and unreleased clinical cohort.

Main Task

For each positive-dose or matched zero-dose episode, predict the future MOAA/S level approximately 60 s after the index time. The index dose, including 0 mg reference episodes, is an input. We will also report whether responsiveness increased, remained unchanged, or decreased relative to the pre-index state.

Inputs:

  • 30 s of pre-index EEG, ECG, photoplethysmography, respiration, and available vital signs;
  • current MOAA/S and its measurement time;
  • index dose, prior propofol doses, cumulative dose, and time since the previous dose;
  • static patient and procedure information known at prediction time;
  • processed EEG parameters;
  • organizer-provided pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic features from the Eleveld model.

Teams may use a subset of modalities. Missing modalities will be marked explicitly.

Data

Training examples will be derived from the public DOSE-I dataset. It contains 171 routine endoscopy recordings, 78.5 h of multimodal data, and repeated clinical responsiveness assessments. A feasibility audit identified 659 candidate positive-dose response episodes across 165 recordings under a provisional definition. Matched 0 mg reference episodes will be added without using future outcomes for matching. Final evaluation will use held-out recordings that are not publicly available.

The underlying DOSE-I dataset is available for download via Zenodo. Accompanying this dataset, we have published a comprehensive technical documentation on arXiv.

The DOSE-I data includes five primary biosignals:

  • ECG is recorded at 500 Hz in mV with a ±500 mV range using a 3-lead configuration.
  • EEG is sampled at 125 Hz in microvolts with a ±187.5 µV range across two channels and multiple electrode pairs.
  • Plethysmography (PLETH), sampled at 125 Hz with a 0–100 range via a finger sensor.
  • Respiration (RESP), recorded at 62.5 Hz using impedance-based measurements.
  • Non-invasive Blood Pressure (NIBP), measured every 3 minutes across systolic, mean, and diastolic values using three channels.
This data is further annotated with state-of-consciousness, MOAS/S, and clinical events. For a sample recording, see below.

Evaluation

The proposed primary score is recording-balanced mean absolute error for future MOAA/S, averaged equally across positive-dose and zero-dose episodes. Secondary results will include direction-of-change macro-F1, rank correlation, and calibration.
